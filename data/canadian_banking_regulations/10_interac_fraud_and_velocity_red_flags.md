## 1. Regulatory Mandate & Operational Summary
Catalogues Interac e-Transfer fraud indicators: rapid velocity burst just under $3,000 threshold (smurfing), sudden new recipient email/phone, device fingerprint mismatch, SIM-swap indicator, and mismatch between declared payee and Autodeposit legal name.

Built-in protection,every time you pay

When you pay with Interac Debit, you’re protected by industry‑leading safeguards at every step. These protections are built in — whether you’re tapping for a quick contactless payment at your local store, or shopping online or in-app, withdrawing cash from an ABM, or making a Chip and PIN purchase in person.

Key protections include:

When you’re shopping on your phone, Interac Debit uses random codes called “tokens” to complete a purchase, to hide your sensitive details and help prevent fraud.

This policy is designed to protect customers who have been targeted by scams related to their Interac Debit card. Your bank or credit union may reimburse you if it finds that a fraud incident was beyond your reasonable control and you have met the criteria, in accordance with your client agreement.

Every transaction is encrypted and unique, making it extremely difficult to copy or counterfeit your card.

Built‑in spending limits for contactless payments help reduce your risk if your card is lost or stolen.

A secure way to send and receive funds

As one of the world’s safest digital money transfer services, Interac e-Transfer offers Canadians the bank-grade security they expect. The transfer happens over secure payment networks that banks use. Money never travels by email or text (only the payment notification and deposit instructions do). Every transaction is encrypted and monitored by a dedicated team that works 24/7 with banks and credit unions to prevent fraud.

Tip: When you use Interac e-Transfer to send money, the recipient’s name will appear before you confirm if they have Autodeposit enabled. Pause there and confirm you’re sending the funds to the right person.

Here’s how we help protect every transfer:

Funds are transferred from the sender’s financial institution to the recipient’s using secure payment networks and encryption technology that hides your sensitive details.

Neither the sender nor receiver will ever see the other party’s banking details. And you only need the recipient’s email or phone number to send money.

For added convenience and security, if you register an email address or mobile number for the Autodeposit feature, funds arriving via Interac e-Transfer will go directly into your account, without the need for a security question and answer.

If Autodeposit isn’t enabled, recipients must first log in to online banking (with a username/password, Touch ID or Face ID) and then answer a security question that only they and the sender know.

How to use Interac e-Transfer safely

Security is a shared responsibility. Here’s how you can help protect yourself:

Set up the Autodeposit feature

Have money deposited straight into your chosen account automatically, so there’s no security question, and less chance of phishing.

Verify before you send

Doublecheck the recipient’s name and contact details.(Tip: If the recipient has a Canadian bank account and is registered for Interac e-Transfer Autodeposit, you will see their full legal name when you select them on your payee list, before you confirm the transaction.)

Use strong security questions

Avoid easy answers that can be guessed or found online.

Keep your devices updated

Install the latest software and security patches.

Protect your banking login

Usestrong passwordsand enable two factor authentication if available.

Be on guard againstphishing

Interac will never ask for your banking password via email or text.

Unexpected Interac e-Transfer notification?

If you receive an Interac e‑Transfer notification you weren’t expecting:

Confirm with the sender using adifferent communication method.

If it’s from someone you don’t know,don’t click any links or respond.

Report suspicious messages tophishing@interac.ca.

If you think you’ve been a victim of fraud, contact your financial institution and theCanadian Anti‑Fraud Centreimmediately.
